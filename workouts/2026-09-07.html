Warmup15 min

10:05 Thirty-Two (3v3 Two-Touch)OS-10 · Competitive · Out of System · 3v3 15min

Today

Warmup game. 10 players in two groups of five: each group fields 3v3 with two waiting, and we use the exchange version (all three rotate off each time the ball crosses) so the two waiting come in every rally. Setters and middles start in middle back, pins in left back and right back. Two touches only, no tips, free ball to the winning side, games to 15 or cap at time. Talk every ball: mine, name the attacker, cover.

How to run

SETUP - 3v3. Liberos and setters in middle back, hitters in left back and right back - Lines for LB and RB on the side of the court, line for middle back behind the end line - Defense in the back court, offense in the front court - All out-of-system attacks go to the pins (outside and right side) RUN IT - Only 2 touches to get the ball over: every dig is also the set - Middle back digs: dig-set to either pin - A pin digs: dig-set must go to the OPPOSITE pin, high enough to be hittable - Free ball entry to the winning side - If a ball truly can't be controlled, playing it over is allowed (middle back may jump-hit the 2nd ball over), but force most balls to the pins

Scaling and variations

- Only 2 touches to get the ball over - No tipping allowed (a free ball over is fine, a jump-tip is not) - Games to 15, ball goes to the winning side, play out every ball - Scoring variation: score only on kills, or score on all points - Exchange version: every time the ball crosses the net, all 3 rotate off and a new trio comes in - Munciana back-row version (called "3-2"): all attacks come out of the back row, make-it-take-it, play to 5 or 7

Coaching keys

- Focus on getting good first touches - Better the ball: move it forward and high - Attack with no fear TALK - Pre-pass: "Mine mine mine" - Post-pass: name the attacker, "cover cover" - Hitters call for the set A5 - Eliminating a contact speeds up every decision; the training effect is thinking faster - OOS balls don't have to be perfect but they must be hittable: "we've been working on this for three months, it should be good" MUNCIANA - "I always wanna be on attack": with two contacts there's no setter to bail you out, every digger is a hitter a heartbeat later - Dig to target, meaning to a spot the next attacker can actually hit out of

Source: A5 Clinic + Munciana Camp (video 1:00:24)

Ball control45 min

10:20 One Arm Small Touch ProgressionBC-01 · Progression · Ball Control · Partners 12min

Today

Five pairs. Beginner series (toss and play) for about 4 minutes to groove arm shape, then advanced (both play) for the rest. If short on time skip skaters, keep V-touches. Watch: flat inside forearm, arm never bends, see the contact, pass with the legs, balance both feet before every contact.

How to run

BEGINNER (one tosses, one plays) - Partners line up across from each other ~10ft - Tosser holds free hand up as the target; passer drives the ball to the partner's head/hand - Passer calls Mine + partner's name on every contact; tosser counts and gives feedback - Feet and arm of passing side forward 1. Right arm only 2. Left arm only 3. Right arm on one leg, then left arm on one leg 4. To yourself: right, left, two arms, then alternate (left, right, two arms) 5. V-touches ADVANCED (both players play the ball, no tossing) - Doubles as ball control + hand-eye training - Stay in a small controlled space; don't let the rally drift deep 1. Right arm to right arm 2. Left arm to left arm 3. Alternating: right, right, left, left 4. To yourself: right-left-two arms, alternate each cycle 5. Skaters: push off laterally (skater jump), pass one-arm off the outside leg, covering as much space as possible. Shoulders in front, straight arm, legs do the work 6. V-touches V-TOUCH MECHANICS - Start at a ball on the floor, in posture - Shuffle back at an angle (two shuffles, painting one side of a V), balance, pass with the OUTSIDE arm - Run forward, touch the ball, then paint the other half of the V the opposite way - Right side back = right foot forward - Cadence: follow ball, slide, hand up

Scaling and variations

Beginner reps: 40 right / 40 left / 20+20 one leg / 20 self-series / 10 V-touches Advanced reps (per pair): 40 right-right / 40 left-left / 40 alternating / 20 self / 10 skaters each / 10 V-touches each Skilled pairs on alternating: one player sends every ball straight, the other diagonal

Coaching keys

- Externally rotate the elbow: play the ball on the FLAT inside part of the forearm, not the side of the arm - Watch the ball hit your platform, every rep. 'You've got to see contact on everything we do in the game' - Arm never bends; hold the finish toward the partner's hand - Balance both feet every time before the contact - Shoulders forward, weight shifts slightly into the pass; pass with the legs, not an arm swing - Keep the shoulder relaxed; a tight, tense shoulder kills the drive - On shuffle-back reps, weight stays on the balls of the feet, never the heels - Talk is graded: player on the ball calls Mine + partner's name; the partner counts - Compact drills keep the little things (balance, arm shape, eyes) visible and correctable on every rep

Source: A5 Clinic

10:32 Gopher 3sBC-07 · Skill · Ball Control · Trios 10min

Today

Trios between the 10 ft line and the net, tossers on the net side. Three trios plus one extra who joins as a second tosser and rotates in. One ball first: 30 seconds tilting left, 30 seconds tilting right, rotate the passer. Add two-ball only for trios that are clean. Key: drop the inside shoulder to tilt, quiet platform, aim past the target's outside hand.

How to run

Setup: trios, run between the 10-foot line and the net. Can have 4 sets of trios on each half of court. 2 Tossers are on the net side or end line side. Passer in trio passing towards net / end line One Ball 1. 2 Tossers are on the net, passer on the 10 foot line. Passer shuffles towards facing Tosser #1. Tosser #1 tosses a ball forward 2. Passer plays a tilt pass, dropping the shoulder closest to target so the ball angles back past the opposite outside hand 3. Target raises the outside hand high as the target and calls 'good' or 'bad' on each rep 4. Run about 20 left, 20 right), then rotate the next passer in Two Ball 1. Each tosser has a ball 2. Tosser tosses a diagnol ball when the passer shuffles away from them 3. Passer performs a tilt pass back to the same tosser

Scaling and variations

- 30 seconds tilting left, 30 seconds tilting right - Basic: start 4 to 5 m off the net so passers have time to shape the platform; keep tosses slow - Advanced: tighten the target window to within a meter of the setter's stance

Coaching keys

- Drop the inside shoulder (the one closest to target) to tilt the ball back, do not swing the platform - Quiet platform: the angle comes from posture, not from chopping the arms - Aim the ball back past the target's opposite outside hand

Source: Munciana Camp (video 1:29)

10:42 Russian PushBC-09 · Skill · Ball Control · Trios 10min

Today

Same trios. One tosser at the 10 ft line, one at the end line, each with a ball. 60 seconds continuous per middle player, rotate so everyone goes twice. Key: beat the ball back, get set and balanced before contact, hands away from the hips between balls, pass to the forehead. Quicken the cadence only for players who reset cleanly.

How to run

SETUP - One passer works between two tossers: one at the 10-foot line, one at the end line, each with a ball - The middle player is the only one working; the tossers feed free balls CONTINUOUS RUN (Coach Song version) 1. Turn to face the first tosser, back up off the ball, and free-ball pass to their forehead 2. The instant you pass, turn and shuffle back to face the other tosser 3. Back up again and pass the second ball the same way 4. Keep alternating: pass, turn, shuffle back, pass the other direction - Always be moving away from the ball so you work to a moving target with hands away from the body ONE-BALL VARIATION - Tosser hits a down ball, passer digs back to the same hitter, she sets it to the opposite hitter, who hits at the digger

Scaling and variations

- 45 to 90 seconds continuous per middle player, then rotate - Aim for the forehead of the target on every pass - Basic: keep the cadence slow enough to fully reset between balls; go one ball at a time if the reset is missing. Start the tossers closer, toss higher and slower, then widen and quicken as form holds - Advanced: quicken the cadence so the second ball arrives mid-recovery - Variation: tossers vary depth (short then deep) so the middle reads distance, not just direction

Coaching keys

- Talk every rep: call the ball, call the target - Beat the ball back: get set and balanced before it arrives, do not pass on the move - Get back from the ball: passers get in trouble reaching forward, so the second ball forces the reset - Hands away from the hips between contacts, no resting in the platform position - Platform out early and quiet; let the legs push the ball, no arm swing - Drop the inside shoulder to angle the ball back to the target - Work to a moving target

Source: Munciana Camp (video 1:44) + Coach Song

10:52 Directional PassingBC-12 · Skill · Ball Control · Stations 13min

Today

Stations as one group. Coach Christen enters the ball, Coach Song at the T watching cover. Start with the back slots (LB, MB, RB) at 45 seconds each, add LF, MF, RF if reads are clean. Mix platform and hand reps. Key: run hard to the dot and arrive at a target, finish in cover every rep (outside foot on the line, belly to target), call mine. Water break after.

How to run

Setup: run as a station. A dot in the left corner; a dot in the middle of the court for target; a dot in middle back at the 15-foot line for target. Three lines: one at target catching, one at the left net getting ready to pass the first ball, one holding balls at the coach. Passers cycle through three start slots (LF, MF, RF, RB, MB, LB). 1. A player throws the ball antenna-high at the target; when the ball line tosses, the next passer immediately sprints to the passing line 2. The left front player pursues, reads, and passes the ball to the dot/target 3. Immediately run to right front to cover, then rotate to the target line 4. Target catches and immediately runs to the back of the throwing line 5. Run about 45 seconds per slot, then rotate. Mix platform reps and hand reps

Scaling and variations

- Run starting from left front, middle front, then right front - Run passing from left, middle, then right back (all six slots) - Entry variations: coach toss / player throw above the antenna / box / down-on-the-floor serves - Basic: start with three slots only (LB, MB, RB); add the front slots once back-row reads are clean

Coaching keys

- Run fast: move hard to the dot and arrive at a target, do not just send the ball somewhere - Always finish in cover: pass, then finish covering the swing, every rep - Call 'mine,' say what you are going to do - Cover: outside foot on the line, belly to target - Forehead over the knee; hands away from the hips - Put a 2nd coach at the T to watch whether the girls cover hard - Munciana: six slots (LF/MF/RF/RB/MB/LB), lots of talk

Source: Munciana Camp (video 4:55)

Hitting35 min

11:05 Hitting ProgressionAT-01 · Progression · Attacking · Partners 35min

Today

Mostly pins, so run stages 1 to 9 in order. Footwork stations: outside and right side lines, middle station for the middles. Wall rotation, kneeling snap, self toss topspin, jump and chuck: about 3 minutes each. Partners across the net: 5 minutes. Coach toss stages 7 to 9: one group of five with Coach Christen, the other with Coach Song, 4 hits per variation so lines stay short. Smooth over power the whole way, listen for the splat, bicep to the ear, wait for the peak then go on the last two steps.

How to run

1. TRANSITION FOOTWORK STATIONS (count off 6s, rotate) - Outside: Start on net, play off-blocker defense, eyes towards the court, step-cross hop. Get to the cone, turn, run outside the cone, take your approach - Middle: Swing block to side, block, transition around the far cone, take your approach - Right side: Jump and block, Transition behind the cone, take your approach. Go around the cone away from the setter's path 2. WALL ROTATION - Hitting shoulder to the wall, right knee up (knee closer to wall) - Elbow pulled back pointing behind you, left arm up high, left shoulder higher - Rotate forward, bring elbow up high and right hand under pony tail. replace left hand with right on the wall, snap at the top (now right shoulder higher), pull back and repeat 3. KNEELING WRIST SNAP (no toss) - Right knee down on a line, elbow back, hit the ball straight out of your hand - Goal: ball comes back on your right side so you catch it with your right hand - Stop the swing at the height of your leg; hand finishes no lower than your higher knee; stay upright 4. STANDING SELF-TOSS TOPSPIN - Toss with the hitting hand, rolling the fingers off the ball for topspin; elbow goes back immediately - Snap straight down so the ball comes back to you - Then thumb-up version: ball travels left, reach out with the left hand to catch it 5. JUMP AND CHUCK THROUGH THE NET - Face right front (toward your setter), reach back, swing up, jump with elbow back - Rotate in the air and throw the ball through the net as a line drive - No chicken wings. Trains rotating your body in the air without the floor to help you turn 6. PARTNERS ACROSS THE NET - Self toss with hitting hand and full draw - Left, Right-Left shuffle - Snap under net to partner - Snap above net to partner 7. UNDER-NET LOW-TOSS SNAP (feed the coach, then hit) - Coach toss is low; shuffle up with elbows back, hit under the net - Restrain the swing-through and work the snap; get the feet ALL the way to the ball 8. FLAMENGO TWO STEP - Coach tosses a 1.5 tempo - Wait for peak of the toss, then go on the last two steps, with pace - Pull back and bring the bicep to your ear; smooth swing, finish with the arm up 9. FULL APPROACH - Timing step (right foot) down; coach tosses about a 1.5 tempo, not very high - Wait for the peak of the toss, then go on the last two steps, urgent - Pull back and bring the bicep to your ear; smooth swing, finish with the arm up - Rounds: straight on, then fingers down, then thumb up targeting area 1 (right-back corner)

Scaling and variations

- Coach-toss stages: everybody hits 4 per variation - Smooth over power the whole way; add power only when the wrist and feet hold up - Self-toss stages: scoot back to the white line for room

Coaching keys

BODY BEFORE ARM - Hips fire first, torso rotates, the arm comes LAST. - Tighten the core as you swing: power transfers through the core, not the arm - Power seat in the air: heels come slightly up as the body loads back, then kick out as the hips snap - Big muscles (legs, hips, torso) generate the power; the arm's small muscles steer the shot - On extreme wrist-away shots: hips face the sideline, throw the right hip through the ball - Hit tall: fully extended at release, never folded small; land on two feet, knees out, never caving in THE LOAD - Load like a center fielder throwing home: elbow even with the shoulders, thumb pointing at your head - Set the attacking elbow into position; don't max-jump with both hands over your head - Bicep to the ear on the swing: a gap between elbow and ear costs contact height THE SNAP - More wrist than arm. If you don't expect your wrist to work, it will never develop - Hand loose and relaxed: the looseness creates the snap. Stiff hands are for serving - Hit QUICK, not hard: tensing the shoulder girdle to muscle the ball slows the arm down - Listen for the SPLAT: no splat off the hand = wrong contact. Check contact by ear - Hit the BOTTOM of the ball for topspin and control; the back of the ball goes into the net - Reach and get on top of the ball; don't drop the elbow SHOTS AND DECEPTION - Fingers down = topspin; thumb up = the cut - Ladder the shots: high, medium, low (low finishes at the ankles), then left, middle, right - Hold the blocker: show cross, cut late; finishing toward the net means you turned too much - No looky cookie on the cross-body shot: no peeking, feel it off your hand - Clear mind: never pre-decide the shot. The set, the block, and your approach dictate it FEET AND RHYTHM - Get your feet all the way to the ball: leaning and reaching = lost power or hitting into the net - Shuffle into every rep with approach rhythm (left, right-left); no flat-footed hitting - Wait for the peak of the toss, then go on the last two steps, urgent. Better late than early HOW TO TRAIN IT - Practice makes permanent: the body defaults to easy mechanics, so quality reps - Scaling rule: if it's simple, make it more complex; if it's complex, make it simple. Strugglers stay at their stage - Smooth before power: add pace only when the wrist and feet hold up

Source: How to Hit Better (video) + Art of Coaching + Front Range VBC + USA Volleyball

Game15 min

11:40 Left v Left (Be Left)TP-12 · Competitive · Team Play · 5v5 15min

Today

Closing game, 5v5, group against group. Three back-row defenders plus a setter in each back row, one blocker at left front, no middle. Coach free ball entry, left front hits cross-court, the other side digs, sets, and counters cross. Make-it-take-it, 3-point games, best of three, then mirror as right v right if time. Keep swings controlled and soften the entry if pace runs high.

How to run

Left-on-left cross-court contest. Score make-it-take-it, 3-point games, best of three per pairing. Setup - Three back-row defenders and a setter in each back row - One blocker up at left front on each side, matched to the cross-court hitter - No middle Rally - Coach enters a free ball to one side - That side passes, sets, and the left front hits cross-court at the other team - The other team digs the hard cross, sets, and counters with its own left front cross-court swing - Play it out, make-it-take-it

Scaling and variations

- Right vs right (mirror) - Add a middle blocker so the cross hitter sees a two-block - Play to 5 with rotation between rallies; Advanced version adds a libero on deep cross and live blocks - For 13-14, the cross-court pace can run high; keep swings controlled or soften the entry

Coaching keys

MUNCIANA - Hitters hunt the hard cross, hitting a one-on-one ball over or around the single block - Diggers sit deep cross; the swing geometry makes the seam and angle predictable - Rewards swing volume and read accuracy, not finesse
